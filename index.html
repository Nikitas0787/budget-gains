Meal-prep guide

Shop once, cook twice, eat well all week

A short guide to making the planner work in a real student week in Cyprus. Read it once, then keep it open on shop day.

1 · Shopping smart in Cyprus
  • Pick one supermarket for the main shop. Splitting a small list across stores wastes time and bus money. Prices for the same basket can differ by around 10% between chains, so compare a few staples you buy every week and stick with the cheapest for you. The government's e-Kalathi price comparison can help.
  • Buy own-brand for staples. Rice, pasta, oats, canned tomatoes, beans, frozen vegetables and tuna are basically the same product for less money.
  • Check the weekly offers before you go. Most supermarkets publish weekly leaflets and app offers. If chicken or yogurt is on offer, swap a meal in the planner to use it.
  • Shop after you eat, with the list. Tick items off on your phone and don't add anything that isn't on it.
  • Use what's in season. Cyprus citrus is cheap through the winter, and local tomatoes, cucumbers and courgettes are cheapest in the warmer months.
  • Compare the price per kilo, not the pack price. It's on the shelf label in small print.
2 · Your weekly cooking session (about 90 minutes)

Do this on shop day or the evening after. It covers the first 2–3 days, and every dinner after that makes the next day's lunch.

  1. Start the slow things (0–10 min). Put lentils or soaked chickpeas on to boil. Start the oven if you're doing a tray bake.
  2. Rice or bulgur (10–25 min). Cook 2–3 days' worth at once. Spread it on a tray to cool fast, then put it in the fridge within an hour.
  3. Boil eggs (while the rice cooks). 6–8 at once for snacks and breakfasts.
  4. Chop once (25–45 min). Onions, peppers and carrots for the next three dinners. Keep them in a tub lined with paper towel.
  5. Cook the first dinner (45–80 min). Make the full 2 portions. Eat one and put one straight into a lunch tub.
  6. Portion and label (80–90 min). Write the day on each tub. Freeze any meat you won't cook in the next 2 days.
3 · Kit you actually need
  • One large pot with a lid, one non-stick frying pan, and one baking tray if you have an oven.
  • 6–8 food tubs with lids (about 750 ml each), microwave-safe.
  • A sharp knife, a chopping board, a wooden spoon and a sieve.
  • Optional: kitchen scales for the first couple of weeks. After that you'll judge portions by eye.

No oven? Choose "Hob only" in the planner and it leaves out oven meals.

4 · Storing food safely
  • Keep your fridge at 5 °C or below. Raw meat goes on the bottom shelf, in a container.
  • Cool cooked food fast and put it in the fridge within an hour.
  • Cooked rice: eat it within 24 hours, and reheat it only once.
  • Other cooked leftovers: eat within 2–3 days, or freeze them.
  • Thaw frozen meat in the fridge overnight, never on the counter.
  • Reheat until steaming hot all the way through.
  • If something smells or looks wrong, throw it out. Food poisoning costs more than the food.

6 · Eating for your goal
  • Building muscle: eat a little more than you burn. Aim to gain about 0.25–0.5% of your body weight a week. If you gain faster, cut some rice, bread or oil. The planner adds extra calories mainly through cheap carbs, not more meat.
  • Maintaining: keep your weight steady and your protein up. This is a good place to start if you're new to training.
  • Losing fat: eat a little less than you burn and keep protein high, so you keep your muscle. Losing 0.5–1% of body weight a week is a sensible pace.
  • Spread your protein across 3–4 meals instead of one big serving.
  • Check and adjust. Weigh yourself 3–4 mornings a week and compare weekly averages. After 2–3 weeks, adjust your calorie target in "About you" if needed.
  • Don't use automatic targets if you're under 18, pregnant, have a medical condition or a history of disordered eating. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian.
7 · Living with flatmates
  • Ask for one fridge shelf and one cupboard shelf, and label your tubs.
  • Cook when the kitchen is quiet, and clean as you go.
  • Share the basics, like oil, salt and spices, and split the cost.
  • Cook together: two people using the same plan can buy bigger packs and pay less each.
8 · The starter cupboard (buy once)

Olive oil, salt, black pepper, oregano, paprika, cumin, cinnamon and garlic powder. Together these come to roughly €12–18 at the estimated prices, and they last for months. If you share with flatmates, it's a few euros each. Recipes in the planner assume you have them.

How it works

What this tool can and can't do

Who made it

A university student in Cyprus who trains and wanted to eat well on a student budget. It's a free student project, with nothing to buy and no ads. It isn't run by a dietitian or a doctor.

Prices are a ballpark

Every price is a ballpark estimate of a typical Cyprus supermarket price, based on Cyprus Consumer Association averages (May 2026). They aren't live prices and they don't come from any particular store. Real prices differ between shops, brands and weeks, so use the totals as a guide, not a quote.

How calories and protein are estimated

Calories use the Mifflin–St Jeor formula with your activity level: 15% less to lose fat, 10% more to build muscle. Protein is 1.6–2.0 g per kg of body weight. These are starting estimates, not prescriptions. Your weight trend, hunger and training tell you more, so adjust after 2–3 weeks or enter your own target.

Who it isn't for

If you're under 18, pregnant or breastfeeding, have a medical condition, or have a history of disordered eating, please don't use these automatic targets. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian instead.

What it promises

The shopping list adds up whole packs at the prices shown and never goes over your budget at those prices. If your budget can't cover your target, it tells you instead of quietly giving you less food. It can't promise results; those depend on your training, sleep and consistency.

Your data

Nothing you type is sent anywhere. Your answers and your current plan are saved only in this browser so they're still there when you come back.
